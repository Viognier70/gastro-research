The Name

Gustème

Gustème — the smallest meaning-bearing unit of taste. Claude Lévi-Strauss coined the word in 1945, proposing that a cuisine can be read like a language: just as speech breaks down into phonemes, a culture's cooking breaks down into units that take on meaning through contrast. Sweet against sour. Raw against cooked. Everyday against feast.

We do the same with research.

Every article is broken into three forms of knowledge, for each professional role. Episteme — what the research establishes. Techne — how it applies. Phronesis — the situated judgment that separates those who know the rules from those who know when to break them.

Lévi-Strauss set gustema against tecnema: the unit of taste against the unit of technique. We add the third. A profession is neither theory nor technique — it is the movement between them, again and again.

Gustème is the concept that sits beneath — taste as a unit of meaning, not only of chemistry. The name Gusto Science says instead what is done here: research, read for the profession.
